Generating Income with Covered Calls: An Introduction to Options for Passive Investors

For long-term equity investors, generating passive yield is usually limited to dividend-paying stocks. However, options allow you to generate extra yield on your holdings. By writing **covered calls**, you can collect cash premiums from buyers, creating a recurring income stream. Here is the covered call blueprint.

1. The Mechanics of a Covered Call Trade

To execute a covered call, you must own at least 100 shares of the underlying stock. You then sell (write) a call option with a strike price above the current market price and set an expiration date (e.g., 30 days out).

The option buyer pays you an upfront cash fee (premium). If the stock stays below the strike price at expiration, the option expires worthless, and you keep both your shares and the premium. You can then write another contract to collect more yield.

"Covered calls turn your stock portfolio into a yield-generating engine, but they require you to accept capped upside in exchange for immediate income."

2. Navigating the Capped Upside Risk

If the stock rises above the strike price at expiration, you are obligated to sell your 100 shares at that strike price. While you capture the gain up to the strike plus the premium, you miss out on any gains above the strike. This strategy is best suited for neutral or slightly bullish stocks where you do not expect sudden price spikes.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

What is a covered call?

An options strategy where you write (sell) a call option on a stock you already own, earning a premium in exchange for agreeing to sell the stock at the strike price if exercised.

What is the main risk of a covered call strategy?

You cap your upside potential; if the stock price skyrockets, you must still sell it at the lower strike price.

When is writing covered calls most effective?

In neutral or slightly bullish markets, where the stock is unlikely to rise above the strike price before expiration.
