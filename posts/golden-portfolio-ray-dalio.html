The All Weather Framework: Compounding Wealth via Ray Dalio's Risk Parity Model

Author: Starrope•June 20, 2026•Read Time: ~5 min
Header Premium Banner

Macroeconomic climates transition through four primary quadrants: rising growth, falling growth, rising inflation, and falling inflation. Most traditional portfolios are heavily biased toward rising growth. To survive all economic regimes, billionaire hedge fund manager Ray Dalio designed the **All Weather Portfolio**. In this guide, we evaluate this risk-parity allocation framework.

1. The Core Principle: Risk Parity over Capital Parity

Traditional portfolios allocate capital evenly (e.g., 50% stocks and 50% bonds). However, because stocks are three times more volatile than bonds, the overall portfolio risk remains over 90% exposed to stock market shocks. Dalio's model uses **Risk Parity**, allocating more capital to lower-volatility assets to ensure each asset class contributes equally to the portfolio's overall risk profile.

"By balancing risk volatility rather than capital allocations, the All Weather framework achieves steady asset growth across all macroeconomic seasons."

2. The Classic All Weather Asset Mix

The standard retail implementation of Ray Dalio's model utilizes a highly structured ETF allocation mix:

US Large Cap Equities 30% VOO / SPY
Long-term US Treasuries 40% TLT
Intermediate Treasuries 15% IEF
Gold (Hard Commodities) 7.5% GLD / IAU
Broad Commodities Mix 7.5% DBC / GSG

3. Performance in Bear Markets

Backtest data spanning over 40 years shows that the All Weather allocation suffers drawdowns far shallower than the S&P 500 index during market corrections. By holding commodities and gold, the portfolio captures gains during high-inflation regimes, while treasuries offset equity corrections during growth shocks.
