1. The Mathematics of Compounding

Simple interest pays earnings only on your principal deposit. Compound interest, however, pays interest on both your initial principal and the accumulated interest from previous periods. Over a short timeframe, the difference seems small. However, as the compounding horizon extends, the growth curve turns vertical (exponential phase), rapidly accelerating capital accumulation.

2. The Rule of 72: A Quick Calculation Hack

To calculate how long it takes to double your investment capital at a specific annual return rate, use the **Rule of 72**. Divide 72 by your expected annual interest rate:

  • At a **6%** annual return: 72 / 6 = **12 years** to double.
  • At a **10%** annual return: 72 / 10 = **7.2 years** to double.
  • At an **18%** annual return: 72 / 18 = **4 years** to double.

3. Starting Early vs. Saving Large Sums

Due to the exponential curve, the time variable is far more important than the deposit amount. An investor who starts compounding small amounts at age 20 and stops at age 30 will typically accumulate more wealth by retirement than someone who starts saving double that amount at age 30 and continues for 30 years.
